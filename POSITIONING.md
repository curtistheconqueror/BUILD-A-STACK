# Build-a-Stack — Positioning

Why this project is not redundant with tech blogs (e.g. makemetechy.com) or
AI/tech communities (e.g. a Skool course). Source notes for future landing /
intro copy.

## The category difference
| | Tech blog (makemetechy) | Community/course (Skool) | **Build-a-Stack** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Format | Articles, how-tos, reviews | Cohort, lessons, chat | **Interactive app you wire & build** |
| Mode | You **read** | You **join / watch** | You **do** (tap-to-wire, explore, click jargon) |
| Source | Generic, SEO-driven | An instructor's curriculum | **A real, running personal stack** |
| Business | Ads + affiliate | Subscription | **Free, offline, no signup** |

The only overlap is the broad goal ("help people get tech-savvy"). That space
competes on content volume + SEO + community. Build-a-Stack competes on a
different axis: **one crafted, interactive artifact grounded in a real system.**

## The five things nobody else has
1. **Taught from a real, living system.** Not "what a webhook is" in the
   abstract — "TradingView → webhook-receiver :3000 → the bot," pointing at
   infrastructure that's actually running. A tour of a working factory by the
   person who built it, not a textbook.
2. **A build, not content.** Constructionist (learn by doing) vs consumption.
   The learner wires the stack, makes mistakes (the buzz), feels it click.
   Stickier — and rare, because it's hard to make.
3. **Self-referential.** The Tunnel card explains the exact Tailscale tech
   delivering the page to the iPad *right now*. The tool teaches what runs the
   tool.
4. **A ladder with a destination.** Tech Noob → Fundamentals → … → analyze your
   OWN stack. An arc, not a pile of articles found via Google.
5. **The "Your Stack" endgame is the moat.** A blog can never map *your*
   machine. A companion that scans a learner's real ports/services and renders
   *their* stack is something none of the alternatives can do.

## What to lean into (the edge)
Double down on the things a blog physically cannot do:
- **Interactivity** (wire it, don't read about it)
- **Real-stack grounding** (every concept maps to something that runs)
- **The "map your own machine" payoff** (Phase G)

The lane is busy with blogs, short on interactivity, and empty of exactly this.
Seeing competitors should confirm the gap, not discourage.
